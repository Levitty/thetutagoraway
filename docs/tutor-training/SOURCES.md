# Sources for the HOREB tutor playbook

Compiled 12 Nov 2026. Everything is summarised in our own words. "Read" means I read the text; "abstract only" means I saw only a summary or abstract, so lean on it less.

## Evidence on effective teaching and tutoring

1. **Principles of Instruction**, Barak Rosenshine, American Educator, 2012. https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/periodicals/Rosenshine.pdf (read). The best teachers present small steps, ask many questions and check every answer, give many examples, guide practice and aim for a high success rate. Silence after "any questions?" is not understanding. Source for: Check for understanding, Show then hand over, Smaller-question hint.
2. **Teaching and Learning Toolkit: one to one tuition, feedback, metacognition**, Education Endowment Foundation. https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit (strand pages via search summaries; figures secondary). One to one tuition adds about five months when it is high quality and matches class teaching. Short, regular sessions work best with younger pupils. Metacognition (plan, monitor, evaluate) adds more. Feedback helps only when it is about the work and the next step.
3. **Wait-time: slowing down may be a way of speeding up**, Mary Budd Rowe, Journal of Teacher Education, 1986. Summaries: https://www.structural-learning.com/post/wait-time-a-teachers-guide (abstract level). Teachers typically wait under a second. When the pause after a question passes about three seconds, answers get longer and better reasoned. Source for: Wait and nudge.
4. **Classroom Discussions: Using Math Talk to Help Students Learn**, Chapin, O'Connor and Anderson, 2009; and the Accountable Talk framework, Michaels, O'Connor and Resnick. Overview: https://www.serpinstitute.org/wordgen-weekly/academically-productive-talk (secondary). The talk moves: revoice (restate and check with the child), press for reasoning ("why do you think that?", for right and wrong answers), add on, wait. Source for: Revoice and build, Press for reasoning.
5. **Feedback: levels of feedback**, Hattie and Timperley, Review of Educational Research, 2007. Summary: https://sites.google.com/ed.act.edu.au/feedback-on-student-learning-w/the-hattie-and-timperley-model (secondary). Feedback about the task and the process beats feedback about the person. Treat the effect sizes with caution (later criticism).
6. **Praise for ability versus effort**, Mueller and Dweck, 1998; APA overview https://www.apa.org/education-career/k12/using-praise . Fifth graders praised for being smart chose easier tasks and persisted less than those praised for effort. A 2020 replication (Li and Bates) did not reproduce the core finding, so the evidence is mixed. We use it as a mild rule: praise what the child did, not what the child is. Source for: Name what was right, Celebrate the strategy.
7. **Worked examples and fading**: Sweller and colleagues; Renkl, Atkinson and Grosse 2004; Kalyuga and Sweller on expertise reversal. Overview https://openlearning.mit.edu/mit-faculty/research-based-learning-findings/worked-and-faded-examples (secondary). Novices learn best from a fully worked example, then steps are removed one at a time. For a child who already knows the idea, the long explanation hurts. Source for: Show then hand over, Stretch the quick child.
8. **Active, Constructive, Interactive (ICAP)**, Michelene Chi, 2009; Chi et al. 2018. https://education.asu.edu/sites/default/files/lcl/chi_et_al-2018-cognitive_science.pdf (abstract level). Learning rises when the learner generates and explains, not when the learner listens. Tutoring works because of what the child gets to do. VanLehn (2011) adds that the gain from human tutors is smaller than often claimed, and that good step-level feedback is what matters. Source for: the child talks at least as much as the tutor.

## Studies of real and simulated tutoring dialogues

9. **MathDial: a dialogue tutoring dataset with rich pedagogical properties**, Macina and colleagues, EMNLP Findings 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536 (read). 3,000 tutoring dialogues in which teachers tutored a model playing a child with a typical error. Teacher moves are Focus (guide attention, ask for next step), Probing (ask why, ask are you sure, vary the question), Telling (reveal a strategy or the answer) and Generic. Teachers mostly scaffolded and moved towards telling only when the child stayed stuck. Off-the-shelf language models gave away solutions too early and marked wrong work as right. Source for: Smaller-question hint, Diagnose the wrong answer, and the main HOREB failure.
10. **Tutor CoPilot: a human-AI approach for scaling real-time expertise**, Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb and Demszky, Stanford, 2024/2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017 (abstract and introduction only; I did not extract the full move list). A randomised trial with 700+ tutors: students whose tutors had expert-modelled suggestions were four points more likely to master topics, nine points for weaker tutors. The suggestions raised probing and guiding questions and cut generic praise ("great job"). Tutors said the language needed adjusting to the child's grade level.
11. **Edu-ConvoKit**, Wang and Demszky, NAACL 2024 demo. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05111 (summary). Measures that research uses on tutoring talk: talk-time ratio, student reasoning, talk moves. Source for: the talk-time target in Turn shape.
12. **CIMA: a large open access dialogue dataset for tutoring**, Stasaski, Kao and Hearst, BEA 2020. https://aclanthology.org/2020.bea-1.5 (summary). Tutor actions coded as question, hint, correction, confirmation. Useful as a vocabulary of tutor turns; it says little about maths errors.
13. **Khanmigo**, Khan Academy blog posts, e.g. https://blog.khanacademy.org/why-were-deeply-invested-in-making-ai-better-at-math-tutoring-and-what-weve-been-up-to-lately/ (company claims, not independent). Hints and questions before the child answers; more direct explanation after a wrong answer; the tutor first works out how the child could have reached the answer; known weakness is judging right and wrong. I could not find Khan Academy's real tutoring guidelines or prompt (only fan copies).

## Fractions

14. **NCETM Teaching for Mastery, Fractions spine (Years 3 to 6)**, segment 3.7 "Finding equivalent fractions and simplifying fractions", plus Upper KS2 fractions guidance. https://ncetm.org.uk/classroom-resources/primm-307-finding-equivalent-fractions-and-simplifying-fractions/ (page summaries; the PDFs were not read in full). Concrete then pictorial then abstract. Equivalence as the same amount with a multiplicative link between top and bottom. Children explain the relationship in words.
15. **Fractions misconceptions**: Queensland QCAA factsheet https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/downloads/aciqv9/general-resources/ac9_gc_num_improving_thinking_fractions_yr2-6_misconceptions.pdf ; NFER on adding fractions https://www.nfer.ac.uk/assessment-hub/what-are-the-common-mistakes-when-adding-fractions/ ; Craig Barton probing questions https://mrbartonmaths.com/?p=4914 ; Eedi blog https://eedi.com/blog/summer-school-insights-week-5-fractions (search summaries). Bigger denominator means bigger fraction, adding tops and bottoms, unequal parts, and getting a right answer for a wrong reason. Eedi data work (diagnostic questions with wrong options tied to named misconceptions) supports the diagnose-by-one-question approach.
16. **Fraction knowledge predicts later maths**, Siegler and colleagues, 2012, via https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3444669 (secondary description). Fraction understanding at 10 to 11 predicts algebra years later; number-line understanding is a strong predictor. Reason to use a number line as well as bars.

## Kenya

The link returned a web page, not the PDF, in my fetch, so I did not read it directly. Its misconception library (22 entries with a question that reveals each and a repair) is the base of the Fractions-specific section.
18. **Lesson shape (introduction, development, conclusion)**. Not confirmed from a KICD text. It is the standard Kenyan lesson-plan format taught in teacher training; I used it for Open and Close, but treat it as an assumption until checked against a KICD teacher guide.
