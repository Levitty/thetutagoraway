## Review list for a CBC science teacher

Please mark each item OK, change or drop. The questions themselves can be seen with `npm run horeb:try` (dev server, all families on); the Maths review pack builder is not used for science. Ids are in the families table above.

**A. Curriculum depth and wording**
1. Flower parts: how far does Grade 5 go? We use petal, sepal, stamen, pistil, with anther, stigma and ovary as extra names. Drop anther, stigma or ovary if the class book does not use them.
2. Non-flowering plants: we use fern, moss, pine and cypress. Is "cone" and "spore" taught at Grade 5, or only "no flowers"? Is a mushroom or alga ever mentioned here (we left fungi out; they are Grade 6)?
3. Pollination, fertilisation and seed formation: we kept to pollen reaching the stigma and the ovary growing into the fruit with seeds. Is this too much or too little for the sub-strand on classification of plants?
4. Vertebrate groups: "structural features only" in the design. We use body covering, how the young are born or fed, and gills or lungs. Confirm that dolphins and bats are taught as mammals at Grade 5, and the amphibian wording ("lives part of its life in water and part on land").
5. The KICD text of breathing outcome c reads "coutline"; we kept it as printed and read it as "outline". Confirm.
6. Breathing: "oxygen and carbon dioxide" and the diaphragm and rib movement. Does Grade 5 name the gases and the diaphragm movement, or only the parts?
7. Mixtures: homogeneous and heterogeneous, and the three types. We treat milk and water as liquid-liquid and salt in water as solid-liquid and homogeneous. Confirm the class book agrees, and that dissolving is taught here.
8. Water: the solar treatment rule (clear water, clear plastic bottle, strong sun, about six hours) and boiling for at least one minute. Confirm the numbers your class uses.
9. Floating and sinking: the design lists shape, weight and size. We say "light or heavy for its size" and use shape and load. Is density or "upthrust" mentioned? (We avoided both.)
10. Sound: we say sound travels fastest through solids, slower through water and slowest through air. Confirm this is taught at Grade 5.
11. Heat: we say hot air rises, heat moves from hot to cold, and that a metal object feels colder because it takes heat from the hand. Confirm "insulator" is the word used.

**B. Facts to check**
12. Dry wood floats; an iron nail, a coin, a key and a stone sink; an empty capped plastic bottle floats; a flat tin lid floats if placed gently. Any item that fails in your classroom should be dropped.
13. A cypress and a pine make cones; a banana plant, an acacia, a jacaranda and a sunflower flower. Check the Kenyan plants named in the plant families.
14. Nile perch, tilapia and shark are fish; crocodile and chameleon are reptiles; flamingo and ostrich are birds; dolphin and bat are mammals.
15. Health wording (breathing illness prevention): "antibiotics do not kill viruses", "a cold is caused by a virus, not cold weather", and the COVID-19 signs. A health worker or the school nurse should confirm before use with children.
16. Safety wording: a wet cloth conducts heat and makes steam; a pot stays hot with no flame; a jiko needs fresh air. Confirm with the school safety lead.
17. Misconception sources are cited from memory (see the library). Verify, or mark "to confirm" and leave them out of anything printed.

**C. Questions and distractors**
18. Read at least one question from each skill. Is every wrong option something a child really thinks, and is the feedback kind and clear? After a wrong answer a child reads the explanation for that question, then the misconception line from the library (the JSON `feedback`); mark any pair that reads badly together.
19. Reading level: sentences are short and the checker allows Flesch-Kincaid up to Grade 7. Mark any question with a word a Grade 5 child in Kenya would not know.
20. Kenyan contexts: shamba, maize, sukuma wiki, githeri, jiko, jua kali workshop, Lake Victoria canoe, dhow, matatu, boda boda, Naivasha flower farms. Add or change any that feels forced.
21. Tables: they are read in words ("Plant A: tall tree with cones"). Say whether Grade 5 children can read a table of this size, or whether a picture is needed.
22. "Spot the mistake" questions (where a child's explanation has one false step): do they feel fair, or too hard before the child has seen the right explanation?
