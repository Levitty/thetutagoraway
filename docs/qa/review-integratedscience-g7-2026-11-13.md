## Keep, with a teacher or health worker note

All other families were true and at level. The items below are kept but must be confirmed before any child sees them (full wording in `TEACHER_QUESTIONS_13NOV.md`):

- First aid (ls.aid.*): cooling a burn with running water, pressing a cut, not making a child vomit after a swallowed chemical, not giving drinks. Standard guidance, but a health worker should confirm the wording and add the time to cool (about 20 minutes).
- Smelling chemicals (ls.acc.*): the items say "never sniff". Many laboratories teach cautious wafting. A teacher should say which the class uses.
- Kidney, skin health and adolescence (ex.hea.*, rp.adc.*, rp.cop.*): facts are standard, but period, pimple and mood advice should be checked by a teacher or health worker, including washed cloth as a pad where pads are not affordable, and when late puberty needs a clinic.
- Male and female reproductive systems (rp.mal.*, rp.fem.*): plain and factual, anatomical words only. Teacher to confirm the vocabulary the design allows (oviduct or Fallopian tube).
- Sublimation of iodine (mx.sep.story): is sublimation in the Grade 7 design, and is iodine used in school laboratories?
- Mass and weight (ap.si.*): everyday Kenyan talk gives "weight" in kilograms. The items treat that as a mistake; a teacher should agree.
- Salt pans near Malindi, Olkaria (geothermal), Masinga (hydro), Lake Turkana (wind): facts are right.
