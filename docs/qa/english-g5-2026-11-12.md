## Findings that matter before anything is switched on

1. **English needs its own marker.** The Maths marker (`answerCheck.js`) drops a leading "a", "an" or "the", ignores punctuation and treats words as terms that can swap places. It marks "few" right for "a few", a sentence without its quotation marks right for one with them, and "than taller" right for "taller than". `src/ai-tutor/answerCheckText.js` is the English marker (exact words, in order, with punctuation; a tapped option matched exactly; capital letters, spare spaces, curly quotes and a final full stop on a single word forgiven when typed). Families carry `marker: 'text'`; the pre-review checks use it. The lesson screen does not call it yet, so it must before an English family is served.
2. **The engine has no English subject yet.** `knowledgeGraph.js` is Maths, so the English families are kept out of the Maths bank (and out of `FAMILIES`), and are not reachable by `generateProblem`. A subject entry, a lesson screen and a skill map for English are still to build.
3. **Comprehension needs the passage on screen.** A comprehension problem carries `passage: { title, text }` (a one-line addition to `families/core.js`). The lesson screen has to show it above the question. Passages are 38 to 110 words so they fit a phone.
4. **Questions about sounds, stress, intonation, speaking and reading aloud** (Listening and Speaking, reading fluency) need a voice tool or a teacher. They are listed as such, not faked with a typed question.
5. **Not built:** Double imperatives (outcomes a and b, second half) (GRAMMA.T8). The design does not define the pattern and the teacher guides were not read. Two imperatives joined by and (Go and fetch water) is the likeliest reading. No family is written until a teacher confirms it.
6. Misconceptions on the reading skills carry generic examples (the pattern of the wrong answer, not one question), because the wrong answers depend on the passage. The families name the misconception behind every wrong choice.

### Rules to confirm (the family treats the first as right)

1. **Must and should** (`M_MODAL_STRENGTH`): "Passengers must wear a seat belt" (law) against "You should wash your hands" (advice). A learner who writes "should" for the law, or "must" for the advice, is marked wrong. Is that the strength taught at Grade 5?
2. **Comparing two things** (`M_ADJ_SUPERLATIVE_FOR_TWO`): "Of the two sisters, Achieng is the taller." Is "the tallest of two" marked wrong in your classroom?
3. **Order of adjectives** (`M_ADJ_ORDER_*`): the design says "size, shape and age". The families use the usual order opinion, size, age, shape, colour: "a big old round stool", "a small red bag". Which order do you teach?
4. **Double quotation marks** (`M_QUOTE_SINGLE`): single marks are valid British style; the design asks for double marks, so single marks are wrong in the families.
5. **Comma before and** (`M_COMMA_SPLICE`): "The bell rang, we ran outside" is marked wrong; "The bell rang, and we ran outside" right. Taught at Grade 5?
6. **Present tense for the future** (`M_FUT_PRESENT_FOR_FUTURE`): used only where it is plainly wrong ("I promise that I help you tomorrow"). Check the other items in the pool; "will" and "shall" are accepted alike.
7. **Since as because** (`M_CONJ_SINCE_TIME`) and **plural possessives** (`M_APOS_PLURAL_OWNER`: "the girls' room"): are they taught at Grade 5?
8. **Few / a few** (`M_QUANT_NEGATIVE_POSITIVE`): the "hardly any" against "some" meaning is tested through the clause after the gap. Is that depth expected?
9. **Demonstratives with a hand**: "Look at ___ books in my hands" has these as the answer and those as a near / far mistake. "Those books in my hands" is grammatical in some voices.
10. **Question tags** (`M_TAG_INVARIABLE`): "isn't it" for every statement is treated as a learner error. Is it common among your learners?
11. **Proverbs**: eight common English proverbs are used. Are these the ones children meet? Add Kenyan English proverbs you teach.
12. **Double imperatives**: not built, because the design does not say what they are (likely "Go and fetch water"). Please say, and a pool can be added.
