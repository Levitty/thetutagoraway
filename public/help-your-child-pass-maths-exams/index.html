How to help your child pass their maths exams

Buying another stack of past papers feels like doing something. But if the foundation is shaky, past papers just rehearse the same mistakes. Here's what actually moves the mark — for KCSE, KCPE and CBC.

Every parent's instinct before an exam is "more past papers." It's not wrong — it's just out of order. Do it too early and you're practising confusion.

The cramming trap

Maths exams reward two things: solid foundations and fluency under time. A child who never mastered fractions can do a hundred past papers and still lose those marks every time — because the problem was never the papers, it was the missing skill underneath.

So the order matters. Fix the foundation first, then use past papers to build speed and learn how the exam asks its questions. Reverse it and you waste the most stressful weeks of the year.

Past papers are for polish, not repair. They sharpen a child who's ready. They can't build the skills a child is missing.

What actually raises the mark

1

Find and close the foundation gaps first

Before revision timetables, find the specific skills that aren't secure — usually from earlier years. Close those, and whole topics that felt impossible suddenly make sense.

2

Short daily practice, not weekend marathons

Fluency is built by frequency. Fifteen focused minutes a day, spaced out, locks skills in far better than a five-hour Sunday that's forgotten by Tuesday.

3

Then drill past papers — under time

Once the foundations hold, past papers do their real job: exam speed, question familiarity, and the habit of showing every step the examiner rewards.

4

Get a tutor for the stubborn topics

There's usually one or two topics that won't yield to practice alone. That's exactly where an hour with a real tutor pays for itself.

Questions parents ask us

How long before the exam should my child start revising maths?

Earlier than most people think. If there are foundation gaps, they take weeks to rebuild, and cramming in the final fortnight can't fix them. Starting a term ahead — with short, daily practice — beats a last-minute marathon of past papers.

Are past papers enough to pass maths?

Only once the foundations are solid. Doing past papers on top of missing skills just rehearses the same mistakes. Fix the gaps first, then use past papers to build exam speed and familiarity.

My child understands the work but still loses marks. Why?

Usually it's fluency and method, not understanding: too slow under time pressure, or dropping marks by skipping the working the examiner rewards. Regular timed practice and showing every step fixes most of it.
