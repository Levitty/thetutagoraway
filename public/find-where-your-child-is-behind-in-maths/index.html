You know something's wrong, but "he's just weak at maths" isn't a plan. Before you can help, you have to find the one thing that's actually missing. Here's how.

The most useful thing you can know about your struggling child is not that they're behind — it's exactly where. And the report card won't tell you.

Why the grade tells you almost nothing

A maths grade is an average, and averages hide the truth. A child on 40% might understand half the syllabus perfectly and be completely lost on the rest — or be a little shaky on all of it. The number looks identical; the fix is completely different.

What you actually need is the specific missing skill — and in maths it's usually not this term's topic. It's a rung from a year or two ago (place value, times tables, working with fractions) that quietly breaks everything built on top of it.

The goal isn't a better mark — it's a location. Find the exact skill that hasn't stuck, and the "he's bad at maths" story usually dissolves.

Three ways to find the real gap

1

Watch them work, out loud

Sit with them on one problem and ask them to talk through each step. Don't correct — just listen for the exact line where it goes wrong. That line is the clue. A child who says "8 times 7 is… 54" doesn't have a division problem; they have a times-tables gap.

2

Work backwards until they're fluent

Give slightly easier questions, then easier again, until they answer quickly and confidently. The point where "slow and unsure" turns into "fast and sure" is roughly where the solid ground ends — and where you start rebuilding.

3

Use a check that does this for you

Doing steps 1 and 2 by hand across a whole subject is a lot. A diagnostic check works down from your child's grade automatically, adapting to each answer until it lands on the first skill that isn't secure.
