If your child is falling behind in maths, the worst thing you can do is pile on more of the same homework — and it's the most common. To fix it, you first have to understand why it happens.

Why children fall behind in maths (it's not laziness)

Maths is a ladder. Every new topic stands on an older one: multiplication needs addition, fractions need division, algebra needs all of it. Miss a single rung — place value, times tables, borrowing when subtracting — and everything built on top of it starts to wobble.

Here's the trap. School moves at the pace of the syllabus, not the pace of your child. A small gap from Grade 3 sits quietly until it quietly breaks Grade 6 work. So your child isn't lost on today's lesson — they're stuck on something from two years ago that nobody caught.

This is why "just do more practice" so often fails. More practice on top of a missing foundation is just more practice at being confused. You have to find the broken rung first.

The 5 things that actually help

1

Find the exact gap, not the symptom

Before any more practice, find the specific skill that never stuck. This one step is what most extra tuition skips — and it's the whole game. A short diagnostic beats guessing every time.

2

Go back, rebuild the foundation, then move forward

Unglamorous, but it's how it works. Fix the missing rung, then climb again. Children move fast once they're standing on solid ground.

3

Little and often beats marathon cramming

Ten to fifteen minutes a day does more than two hours on a Sunday. Maths runs on memory and fluency, and both are built by short, regular practice.

4

Bring in a person when it won't click

Some knots need a human who can watch the working and see the exact line where it breaks — something a worksheet can never do.

5

Take the fear out of it

Praise effort, not being "clever". Treat mistakes as information, not failure. A child who's scared of getting it wrong can't think clearly enough to get it right.
